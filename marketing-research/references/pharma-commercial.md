# Pharma Commercial Positioning Track

Use this track for a drug, biologic, vaccine, diagnostic or medical device, at launch or across the lifecycle. It replaces Modules 0 to 5 in SKILL.md with Modules P0 to P5. The writing rules in SKILL.md still apply.

Pharma positioning differs from general B2B in four ways:

1. **The label sets the limit.** Every promotional claim must be consistent with the approved prescribing information. Off-label promotion is prohibited.
2. **Evidence is the proof.** Claims rest on clinical data, not testimonials. Comparative claims generally need head-to-head evidence.
3. **Several customers, none of them the whole decision.** The prescriber chooses, the payer controls access, the patient adheres, and the pharmacist or specialty pharmacy dispenses.
4. **Everything goes through review.** Promotional material passes medical, legal and regulatory review before use.

---

## TRACK RULES

Apply these on top of the writing rules in SKILL.md.

1. **Never invent clinical data.** No efficacy rates, p-values, hazard ratios, trial names or safety figures unless the user supplied them or a cited public source states them. Write `[DATA NEEDED: ...]` where a figure belongs.
2. **Separate promotional from non-promotional.** Label every draft claim as one of: on-label promotional, payer health care economic information, scientific exchange for medical affairs, or not usable. Off-label and pipeline content belongs only to medical affairs.
3. **Flag comparative claims.** Any "better than", "first", "only" or "best" claim gets a note on the evidence it needs. Cross-trial comparisons do not support superiority claims.
4. **Keep fair balance.** Benefit messaging needs matching risk information. Note where Important Safety Information must appear.
5. **Mark everything as draft.** Close each module with: "Draft for internal strategy. All claims require medical, legal and regulatory review before use."
6. **Use public sources for landscape work.** FDA labels and approval letters, ClinicalTrials.gov, company investor materials, published trials, ICER reports, treatment guidelines such as NCCN, and reputable trade press. Cite them.
7. **Default to US rules.** If the market is outside the US, say which rules differ, for example that most countries prohibit direct-to-consumer advertising of prescription drugs.

This track supports strategy work. It is not regulatory or legal advice.

## STEP 0: PHARMA INTAKE

Ask all at once. Skip anything already answered.

```
To set up the positioning research, I need:

1. Product: name or code, modality, mechanism of action, route and dosing.
2. Indication: approved or expected label, line of therapy, patient population.
3. Stage: pre-launch (phase and expected approval), launch year, growth,
   mature, or loss-of-exclusivity defense.
4. Evidence: key trials and endpoints, and any head-to-head data.
   Paste what you can share or point to public sources.
5. Competitors: current standard of care, direct competitors, and pipeline threats.
6. Market: country or region, and the customers that matter most
   (specialists, primary care, payers, hospitals or IDNs, patients).
7. What you need: full positioning, a single module, or a competitor comparison.
```

## MODULE P5: COMPLIANCE CHECK AND STRATEGIC LENSES

*Goal: Pressure-test the messaging, then find fresh angles that stay within the rules.*

### Compliance Pre-Check

Review every claim from P1 and P4:

| Claim | Consistent with Label? | Evidence Level | Comparative or Superlative? | Fair Balance Needed | Route |
| ----- | ---------------------- | -------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------- | ----- |

Route each claim to promotional, payer communication, medical affairs or rejected. This pre-check prepares material for formal review. It does not replace it.

### Strategic Lenses

Pick 3 or 4. Give one compliant messaging example for each.

- **Disease state reframing:** Change how prescribers see the burden of disease or the cost of inadequate control. Unbranded disease education follows its own rules.
- **Treat to target:** Anchor on a clinical goal the standard of care often misses.
- **Patient type framing:** Define the "right patient" so clearly that the choice feels obvious.
- **Simplicity and burden:** Dosing, monitoring or administration burden the product removes.
- **Access confidence:** Remove fear that patients cannot get the drug.
- **Inertia cost:** What persists for patients who stay on the current regimen.
- **Peer and guideline adoption:** Guideline inclusion and peer use, within the rules for citing them.

| Lens | Reframed Angle | Messaging Example | Claim Type |
| ---- | -------------- | ----------------- | ---------- |

Close with **Top 3 Priority Angles**: one sentence each naming the angle, the audience and the evidence it needs.

## OUTPUT TEMPLATES

### Brand Positioning Canvas

One page: product and indication, target prescriber and patient type, positioning statement, top 3 differentiators with evidence, core message by audience, top 3 objections, evidence gaps to close, and the claims that need review.

### Competitive Brief

The Module P0 landscape map plus one paragraph per competitor on how to position against them within the label.

### Launch Readiness Questions

10 to 15 open questions the brand team must answer before launch, grouped by evidence, access, field readiness and analytics.
